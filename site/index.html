The biggest crypto theft on record

It is more than four times the Bybit hack of February 2025, which until now was the biggest theft from an exchange. The FBI blamed North Korea for that one.

    How they did it

    An exchange keeps most of its customers' money offline. It keeps a smaller amount in online "hot wallets" so it can pay withdrawals quickly, and its software signs each payment out of them automatically.

    Binance said early on 30 October that the attacker got in through one of its engineers' laptops, reached the system that sends withdrawals, and used it to send their own (reported by FinanceFeeds). It says its private keys were not stolen. The blockchains fit that: every payment to the attacker was signed by Binance's own wallets, in the usual way.

    On Ethereum the attacker went further. They got Binance's system to sign 50 copies of a permission that handed control of one wallet to a program they had written. One copy matched the wallet's next transaction number, and three minutes later the program emptied the wallet in a single transaction: 167,464 Ether and 63 other tokens, for a fee of less than a dollar.

    The payments were not built like Binance's normal ones. In the Ethereum block where a second wallet was emptied, Binance's own payments paid the fee its software always pays, and the nine payments to the attacker paid more than twice that. Every amount taken was rounded to one decimal place, such as 7,636.8 bitcoin. That looks like the attacker's software making the requests and Binance's system signing them.

    They had been preparing for at least a day and a half. The first money for the attack came out of Tornado Cash, a service that hides where coins came from, on the morning of 28 October. The next afternoon they rehearsed the Ethereum trick on wallets of their own.

    TRM Labs, a blockchain analysis firm, says North Korea's TraderTraitor group did it and that it is highly confident. The blockchains alone can't show who it was.
